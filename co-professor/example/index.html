What the paper sets out to do, as the engine read it

The paper models a monopolist launching an experience product in which early purchases produce the public quality report. Consumers can pay to delegate the monitoring that keeps a later purchase open. The claimed contribution is a feedback loop. Cheaper delegation shrinks the early cohort, and the smaller cohort lowers the value of delegating. Because a common launch price makes recruiting agent users costly, profit and total welfare can fall. Under preconfigured activation the loss falls on the merchant; under after-offer activation consumers gain at the same time. The stated boundaries are independent reports, saturated evidence and flexible regular pricing. The OM department will judge it against its interest in "the role of technology, people, and society in shaping contemporary business operations" and its call for "rigorous analysis and ... substantial managerial insights."

The engine's summary

The analysis looks technically strong. Every pricing result is global, the proof audit found no errors in the headline results, and the quoted numbers reproduce. What holds the paper back at Management Science's OM department is framing more than mathematics. The abstract leads with the preconfigured result. That result needs consumers who are identical when they sink a setup cost before seeing the offer or their own tastes, and the manuscript's own lemma shows it fails under known heterogeneity (F1). The after-offer result is the robust one, so I would lead with it. The merchant-facing insight that OM asks for is present but buried under hedges and many extensions (F2, F11). After that, most fixes are local. They include sharper abstract qualifiers (F4, F5, F12), the margin condition behind the saturation reversal (F7), a motivation for the common launch price (F15), notation clean-up (F8), and supplying the scripts the companion cites (F9).

Reasons for the verdict

  1. No finding is fatal and no finding was refuted; the proof audit found no sign, algebra or completeness error in any headline result.
  2. F1 is a confirmed structural finding on the result the abstract and introduction lead with: the preconfigured, merchant-borne welfare loss depends on consumers being identical when they activate, and the manuscript's own lemma shows that known heterogeneity at activation removes the deterministic-price equilibrium. Under the ladder, a counted structural finding on the main result means not yet.
  3. F2 is structural but unverified at medium confidence, so it counts as fixable; it still matters for the OM department's call for managerial insight. Fit was judged plausible, so the venue rule does not apply.
  4. The path is concrete and mostly a reframing: lead with the after-offer result, which holds with known tastes and a deterministic global price, state the identical-consumer requirement for preconfiguration, and restate the contribution around the merchant's launch decision (F1, F2), then work through the fixable items F3 to F19.
  5. No finding is refuted, and fit is plausible, so the first rung (CONSIDER_OTHER_VENUE) does not apply.

Positioning

Contribution

Claimed: when early consumption produces the public quality report, cheaper delegated monitoring can shrink the early cohort that supplies that experience. The merchant, facing a committed regular price, keeps a shrinking manual cohort rather than offer a deeper common discount, so profit and total welfare fall. With preconfigured activation, consumers' net surplus is unchanged. With after-offer activation, consumers gain while welfare can still fall. The boundaries are independent reports, saturated report technologies and flexible regular pricing. Supported: the ingest records full proofs for all propositions. The homogeneous preconfigured loss (Prop at line 507) holds on an interval g_* < f < h, which exists only under condition (line 495), and it uses weak, zero-surplus participation. The positive-rent, known-taste, fixed-information and nonlinear-report results are local: small dispersion, compact cost intervals, specified equilibria. The abstract's statement that activation timing and pricing 'determine who gains and who loses' (K12) is a synthesis, not a single result. The flexible-pricing and future-demand boundaries are proven only in the companion.

Novelty

Measured against its own bibliography, the paper combines two mechanisms it calls established. The first is launch pricing when early sales determine consumer-generated quality information (yu2016strategic, papanastasiou2017dynamic). The second is costly monitoring and adverse effects of cheaper information acquisition (moon2018monitoring, branco2012search). What it adds is the feedback loop: voluntary adoption of a waiting-cost-reducing technology changes the early cohort, the cohort changes the return to adoption, and the merchant's common-price recruitment decision determines whether the loop lowers total surplus. Supporting this are the matched fixed-information comparison and the activation-timing comparison, which separate the evidence-production channel and its incidence. That delta seems real and well isolated. Whether it reads as substantial will depend on how sharply the introduction states it against yu2016strategic and branco2012search. New-technology test

Reframe

I replaced the shopping agent with an older service that lowers the per-task cost of monitoring for new evidence, such as a price alert, a wishlist notification or a human personal shopper. No assumption, mechanism or result becomes unavailable: agents differ from manual consumers only in monitoring cost (0 vs h) and setup cost f, and the manuscript states the mechanism 'does not require preferences or errors specific to large language models' (L112).   2. Alternatively, make an agent-specific feature load-bearing: for example, purchase execution triggered by reports, or agents' synthesis of evidence that changes report informativeness.
