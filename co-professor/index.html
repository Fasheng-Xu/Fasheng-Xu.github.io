Explore the Readiness Review: report, examples and proposed pricing

Fix it before the referees find it

A focused pre-submission review for your target journal and department. The report can say “not yet” or suggest another venue. Findings state the checks performed and their limits.

  1. Readiness verdict

    Ready, ready after fixes, not yet, or consider another venue. In words, never as a predicted acceptance probability.

  2. Department fit and desk risks

    Checked against the department's own published statement, for example a request for substantial managerial insight.

  3. Likely referee concerns

    Each labeled fatal, structural or fixable, tied to a location, with the verdict re-derived by an independent pass.

  4. Contribution and positioning

    Options for framing the contribution, with the closest prior work drawn from verified bibliographic sources.

  5. Technical audit

    For the automated analytical review: model assumptions, derivations, comparative statics and figures checked against the supplied source and code. Findings state their evidence, confidence and limits.

  6. Citation integrity

    References checked against Crossref and OpenAlex records, plus a check that key citations support what you attribute to them.

  7. Revision plan

    Sentence and paragraph edits in suggest mode, with your original kept as a commented block. Structural changes come as instructions.

  8. Format compliance and backup venues

    Page and abstract limits, plus two backup venues with their resubmission rules.

  9. Disclosure statement and ledger

    Neutral AI-use disclosure text for the target journal and a record of the tools and review stages used.

  10. What this review cannot do

    The limits, stated plainly. A clean audit is not a certificate that the paper is correct.

Prepare a teaching projectSee how we work with you

You direct the work. Agents help carry it out.

Build practical AI skills through real academic tasks. We help you choose a workflow, give it the right context, and check the result before you use it.

  1. Start with your goal

    Bring a paper, course or proposal. Explain the question, audience, constraints and what a useful result would look like.

  2. Build the context

    Share the materials and preferences that matter. For a paper, that might be methods and target journal; for a course, student level and learning goals.

  3. Work in checkable steps

    Use agents for tasks such as organizing materials, drafting examples and running defined checks. Inspect the evidence and revise the approach as you go.

  4. Make the decisions

    Verify the output, choose what to change, and keep responsibility for the research and teaching. Your questions, judgment and research taste remain central.

Personal means relevant context. Your methods, writing preferences, learning objectives and prior decisions should shape the next task. We work toward context that you can inspect, correct and choose to reuse.

Our aim is to make capable AI workflows more accessible to business professors, with practical lessons and hands-on guidance. Service access follows the regions supported by our providers.

Your work, your decisions

  • You choose the context to share and review the outputs before using them in a paper, proposal or classroom.
  • Paper reviews are automated by default. Human review requires your permission, including a Panel-Signed Review or a specific support request.
  • Before sharing materials, agree the access and retention terms for your workflow. Provider retention and storage of your uploads are separate.
  • Research support is for authors and authorized collaborators. Manuscripts received in confidence for peer review are not accepted.
  • Not affiliated with INFORMS, any journal or your institution. You follow the disclosure requirements that apply to your work.

Where your expertise stays central

  • You choose the research questions and decide what the evidence supports.
  • You set the learning goals and decide what belongs in your classroom.
  • You verify proposed solutions, citations and generated materials.
  • Feedback carries uncertainty. It does not certify correctness or predict acceptance.
  • You approve the final revisions, teaching materials and use of AI.
