What is in a Readiness Review for an analytical paper?

A prioritized report, evidence for each finding and a revision plan you can review. The review works from your LaTeX source and any figure code you include.

Readiness and fit
A qualitative assessment, including “not yet” or “consider another venue”. Journal and department fit, contribution, and concerns ranked by severity.
Technical checks
Assumptions, derivations, comparative statics and figures. Each finding identifies its location, evidence, confidence and, where relevant, the limits of the check.
Citations and submission rules
Bibliographic checks, support for key claims, format requirements and possible backup venues. Always verify the current journal rules.
Actionable revision files
A browser-readable report, AI-use disclosure text and a record of review stages. When suggested edits apply, marked-up LaTeX keeps your originals and a diff shows the changes. Nothing is applied without you.

For example, a Management Science submission is read against the department you name. A clean audit does not certify correctness or predict acceptance.

Teaching

Teach the course
you want to teach.

Your students already use AI. We bring course-design skills and agentic workflows that help you decide what students should practice themselves, where AI belongs and how to build the course around that decision. You review everything before it reaches students.

Redesign for a classroom with AI.

Decide what students should practice on their own and where AI belongs. Our redesign method builds on the two-lane approach from published assessment guidance: a few secured assessments show what each student can do alone, and the rest are open to AI and designed for learning. Activities, assessments and rubrics then ask students to explain and defend their reasoning.

Make the next class work.

Develop lectures, slides, worked examples, assignments and rubrics around a clear learning goal. Agentic workflows draft; you review, test and decide what students see.

Give the course a through line.

Shape scattered notes into a coherent course package, with a clear sequence, consistent terminology and an instructor guide.

Let students make the decision.

Create a custom classroom simulation with roles, decision rounds and instructor controls. Use the debrief to connect what happened in the room to the ideas in the course.

Teaching pilots work from your course materials only: no student work, grades or rosters. We do not use AI-detector scores, and we do not promise that an assignment cannot be done with AI or that learning will improve.

Workflows

Learn the workflow.
Keep the judgment.

Practical lessons and hands-on guidance with the skills and agentic workflows we develop, applied to your own research or teaching. We help you choose the tools, give them useful context and check what comes back.

  1. Start with your goal

    Define the question, audience and constraints. Decide what a useful result would look like.

  2. Build the context

    Choose relevant materials, methods and preferences. Inspect and correct the context before reusing it.

  3. Work in checkable steps

    Give agents defined tasks. Examine evidence, test outputs and adjust the workflow as you learn.

  4. Make the decisions

    You own the questions, research taste and learning goals. Verify the work and choose what to use.

How this differs from what you already have

You may already have a campus AI license, an AI review tool, an editor you trust or a teaching center. Each is useful. Here is what Co-Professor adds.

  1. A general chatbot or campus AI license.

    Good for many questions, and you may already have one. Co-Professor adds structure. For analytical papers: fact-checked profiles of journals and departments built from their published statements, citations looked up in bibliographic records, and a separate pass that tries to refute each finding before you see it. For courses: a staged redesign method in place of a blank prompt. Workflow guidance also helps you get more from the license you already have.

  2. AI review and verification tools.

    Several check a manuscript line by line, and some do it well. We see them as a complement. For analytical papers, Co-Professor works from your LaTeX source and figure code, reads the draft against the criteria of the journal or department you are targeting, and returns a revision plan you carry out. It also reaches beyond the paper, to proposals, courses and the way you work.

  3. An editor or consultant.

    A skilled human reader is worth having, and we do not replace one. Generic editing services rarely specialize in analytical OM, IS and OR models. Co-Professor is built by a business-school professor for business-school research and teaching and, where you want it, leaves you with workflows you can run yourself. Each pilot is scoped and priced with you before it starts.

  4. A teaching center workshop or the AI features in your course platform.

    Workshops teach principles, and built-in tools draft materials. Co-Professor works through your own course with you: which assessments show what students can do alone, where AI belongs, and the activities, rubrics and AI policy that follow.

What does a pilot involve?

You describe the project in a few lines. Work starts only after you agree. A pilot is one defined project, such as one paper, one proposal, one course redesign or a short series of workflow sessions. At the end we ask what was useful and what was not.

How much does a pilot cost?

We set the price with you for each pilot project before work starts. There is no checkout on this page, and nothing is charged unless you have agreed on the scope and the price first. Some projects depend on AI services that are offered only in the regions their providers support.

Who verifies the work and makes the decisions?

You do. Check proposed revisions, citations, solutions and teaching materials before you use them. Co-Professor does not write your paper or proposal; you keep the contribution and the intellectual choices. Follow the AI-use and disclosure rules of your journal, funder and institution.
